3.6 How adults in trusted roles can manipulate and influence children

NCFE specification p.50

Core exam · Section B

Core exam

A position of trust is any role that gives an adult power over a child — teacher, practitioner, coach, volunteer, student, family member. That power can be used to care, or to manipulate and influence so the child is harmed and kept silent. The specification routes are interfamilial abuse, inappropriate games, online materials and communication, making a child observe the sexual behaviour of others, and threats of harm to the child or their family. Professional boundaries exist because the child cannot freely refuse an adult they depend on.

Must know

  • A position of trust is built on inequality: the adult grades, cares, feeds, transports, comforts and is believed. Children want to please that adult. That hold can be abused even when the adult 'means well'.
  • Interfamilial abuse: a parent, step-parent, sibling, cousin or other family member uses the trust of family life — overnight care, shared rooms, 'don't tell, you'll split the family' — to harm and silence the child.
  • Inappropriate games: tickling, play fighting, dares, 'secrets' games or sexualised play that the child cannot easily refuse because an adult or older person is running them.
  • Online materials and communication: sending or showing sexual or otherwise inappropriate content; private messaging, gaming chat, personal social media; moving a child from a public platform to a private one.
  • Observing sexual behaviour of others: exposing a child to sexual activity or images as a way of normalising harm, shocking them into silence, or preparing further abuse. Record and refer; do not describe graphic detail.
  • Threats of harm to the individual or family: 'if you tell, your mum will be sent away / your brother will get hurt / you will be taken into care'. Threats explain why children recant or appear loyal to the person harming them.
  • Students, volunteers and supply staff occupy a position of trust. 'I am only on placement' is not a defence. Breaches go to the DSL / head and, where it is a staff allegation, the LADO — they are not staffroom gossip.

How manipulation uses the role

  • Interfamilial abuse is often hidden by loyalty and by the child's fear of breaking up the family. Settings see the effects (withdrawal, sexualised behaviour, fear of going home) rather than the act. Believe the possibility; do not assume 'nice families' do not harm.
  • Inappropriate games and 'jokes' test what a child will accept. An adult who needs a child to sit on their lap, who dares children to remove clothing, or who runs secrets as a game is using play as cover. Policy on touch, intimate care and open-door working exists for this reason.
  • Online, the same adult can extend the role beyond the building: a personal account, a late-night message, a link, a request for a picture. KCSIE (2026) treats this as a safeguarding and allegations issue, not a 'blurred boundary'.

Silence, threats and the professional response

  • Exposure to the sexual behaviour of others, and threats against the child or people they love, are methods of control. They create shame and fear, which is why a child may defend the adult or refuse to say more. That is not evidence that nothing happened.
  • The practitioner's job is not to prove who did what. Listen, record the child's words, do not promise secrecy, do not ask leading questions, tell the DSL the same day, and do not alert the alleged source of harm. If a colleague is the person of concern, use the allegations procedure and the LADO.
  • The Sexual Offences Act 2003 still matters in the wider legal picture: sexual activity with a 16- or 17-year-old by a person in a specified position of trust is an offence even though 16 is the age of consent. Professionally, the duty starts well before a criminal threshold.

Legislation and guidance

  • Sexual Offences Act (2003)

    Position of trust offences; sexual activity with 16- and 17-year-olds in specified roles is an offence.

  • Keeping Children Safe in Education (2026)

    Staff behaviour, online safety, allegations and the expectation that all staff occupy a position of trust.

  • Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026)

    Intra-familial harm and the multi-agency response.

  • Children Act (1989)

    Welfare paramount; significant harm includes sexual and emotional abuse.

Exam moves

  • Define position of trust as power and influence, then name two specification methods (interfamilial, games, online, observing sexual behaviour, threats).
  • Keep sexual content professional and non-graphic — the examiner wants the method of manipulation, not a description of acts.
  • Apply: if the scenario is a family member, do not only write about teachers; if it is a popular TA, still put the child first.
  • Finish with DSL / do not investigate / do not promise confidentiality.
