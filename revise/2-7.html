2.7 Using current, suitable technology to support learning

NCFE specification p.45

Core exam · Section A (with Element 6)

Core exam

Technology supports education when it is current, age-appropriate and purposeful: monitoring progress, sharing information, offering a variety of media, designing and delivering materials and assessments, widening SEND access, enabling safe collaboration, preparing for future learning and careers, and building digital literacy. A tablet is not automatically good pedagogy. Inappropriate, out-of-date or unsafe use creates gaps, data risks and safeguarding issues.

Must know

  • Accurate monitoring of progress: digital assessment, learning journals and trackers can show gaps quickly if data are entered and interpreted well.
  • Sharing information: secure platforms let teachers, TAs, SENDCos and families see the same observations, targets and homework.
  • Variety of media: audio, video, animation, simulations and interactive texts support different ways of accessing content.
  • Planning, designing and delivering materials and assessments: teachers use software to sequence lessons, create quizzes and adapt tasks.
  • Accessibility for SEND: text-to-speech, speech-to-text, captioning, switch access and overlay tools can remove barriers (Equality Act 2010).
  • Safe online communication and collaboration: filtering, monitoring, age-appropriate platforms and KCSIE duties.
  • Gets learners ready for later study and work: use real tools, not only closed games.
  • Digital literacy: evaluating sources, knowing how AI works, understanding privacy and behaving respectfully online.

Progress, information, media and design

  • Up-to-date systems (for example a current EYFS learning journal or a school MIS) let practitioners record observations against the right framework and spot a child whose communication is not moving. Accuracy depends on professional judgement: a click-box that is never moderated is not assessment. Used well, the same system shares a Reception child's profile with the Year 1 teacher and with parents who cannot attend at 3.30 pm.
  • A variety of media matters because a single worksheet excludes some learners. A KS2 history enquiry can combine a short film, a map, a podcast clip and a museum app; a nursery can photograph block play and play the sequence back so children review (HighScope). Technology here serves cognition, not entertainment.
  • Planning and assessment tools help adults design sequences and low-stakes quizzes that give fast feedback. Generative AI can draft a differentiated resource, but the practitioner remains accountable for accuracy, bias and pitch. Out-of-date software that cannot open a pupil's access arrangement, or a platform the LA no longer supports, fails the 'up-to-date and appropriate' test.

SEND access, safety and collaboration

  • For SEND, technology is often a reasonable adjustment: a Year 8 pupil with literacy difficulties using read-aloud and a laptop in class; a child with physical needs using a switch to choose symbols; captioned video for a pupil with hearing loss. The Equality Act 2010 requires settings not to discriminate; appropriate technology is one way barriers are removed. It does not replace human relationships or a well-pitched task.
  • Safe online communication is a safeguarding duty, not an ICT extra. Keeping Children Safe in Education requires filtering and monitoring, staff who can act on alerts, and teaching that matches the age — from 'who can take your photo' in Reception to deepfakes and generative AI in KS4. Collaboration tools (shared docs, class blogs, video calls with a partner school) are powerful only on platforms the setting can supervise.
  • Home contact must follow GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018: no progress photos on a personal phone, no unsecured WhatsApp groups for a class. A nanny or childminder using a parent app still needs consent and a secure device. Inappropriate technology includes any channel that staff cannot monitor or that exposes a child's image or data.

Future learning, careers and digital literacy

  • Using real tools — spreadsheets in KS2, CAD in KS3 design, data loggers in science, assistive software that pupils will meet in college — prepares children for the next stage and for work. Closed, cartoonish apps that cannot transfer a skill do less of that job. Careers education (Gatsby benchmarks in secondary) includes digital workplaces; ignoring technology leaves pupils unprepared.
  • Digital literacy is the curriculum content that makes the tools educational: searching with a question, checking a source, knowing that an AI chatbot can hallucinate, protecting a password, and speaking respectfully in a shared document. Without it, technology can spread misinformation and harm. Teach it in context — evaluating websites in KS2 geography, referencing in KS4 — not as a one-off assembly.
  • Appropriate also means the child's stage. A toddler needs limited, adult-joint screen time that extends play, not a tablet as a pacifier. A Year 11 needs access that includes unsupervised research with taught criticality. The same device can support or undermine education depending on the adult's purpose, the software's currency and the safety wrap-around.

Legislation and guidance

  • Keeping Children Safe in Education (2026)

    Requires schools and colleges to manage online safety, including filtering and monitoring, as part of safeguarding.

  • Data Protection Act / UK GDPR (2018)

    Governs how settings store, share and delete children's digital information and images.

  • Equality Act (2010)

    Requires reasonable adjustments; accessible technology can be part of removing barriers for SEND.

Exam moves

  • Do not write 'technology is engaging' as a full answer — name a spec purpose (monitoring, SEND access, digital literacy) and an example.
  • Up-to-date means current software, current safeguarding settings and current professional knowledge (including AI), not a 2012 CD-ROM.
  • Appropriate means matched to age, need and task, and safe — a secondary collaboration tool is not automatically right in a baby room.
  • Finish with impact on the child's learning or safety, and the adult's remaining responsibility.
