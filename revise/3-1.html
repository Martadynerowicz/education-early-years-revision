3.1 Why current laws and statutory guidance shape setting policies and procedures

NCFE specification p.47

Core exam · Section B

Core exam

Statute creates the legal duty; statutory guidance tells settings how to meet it. Policies and procedures in early years and schools are the local translation of both. Exam answers must name the Act or guidance, the year, the duty it creates, and how that duty appears in everyday practice — a wet floor, a bruise, a photograph, an EHCP, a Prevent concern.

Must know

  • Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (HASAWA): employers must protect employees and the public (including children and visitors); employees must protect themselves and each other, so far as reasonably practicable.
  • Statutory health and safety: Health and safety: responsibilities and duties for schools (2022) and the EYFS safeguarding and welfare requirements turn HASAWA into setting-level risk assessment, first aid, premises and supervision.
  • Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015: Prevent duty — specified authorities, including schools and registered early years providers, must have due regard to the need to prevent people being drawn into terrorism. Prevent duty guidance for England and Wales (2023) is the how-to.
  • Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR: personal data must be used fairly, lawfully and transparently; recording, sharing and storing (learning journals, photographs, medical and safeguarding files) must have a purpose and be secure. Safeguarding sharing is permitted where necessary to protect a child.
  • Children Act 2004: multi-agency working to keep children safe; local safeguarding children partnerships and a duty to cooperate. Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006: suitability checks (DBS / barred lists) so adults who pose a risk cannot work with children.
  • Children and Families Act 2014 Part 3: SEN and Education, Health and Care Plans (EHCPs) 0–25. Female Genital Mutilation Act 2003: FGM is a criminal offence; regulated professionals have a mandatory reporting duty for known cases in under-18s.
  • Safeguarding statutory guidance that must shape policy: Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026); Keeping Children Safe in Education (2026); Multi-agency statutory guidance on FGM (2020); Supporting pupils at school with medical conditions (2015).
  • Equality and diversity: Equality Act 2010 (protected characteristics, reasonable adjustments, Public Sector Equality Duty); SEND Code of Practice 0–25 (guides for early years and schools); UNCRC 1989 (rights of the child, including non-discrimination, voice and protection).

Health, safety, security and confidentiality

  • HASAWA 1974 is not a poster. It requires competent people, risk assessment, first aid, safe premises and, where five or more people are employed, a written health and safety policy. Employees share the duty: they follow procedures, report hazards and do not put others at risk. The 2022 schools duties guidance and the EYFS welfare requirements make that concrete (ratios, medicines, outings, premises).
  • Security and confidentiality sit on two statutes. The Prevent duty (2015 Act; 2023 guidance) is met through British values, age-appropriate curriculum, a culture where concerning isolation or language is noticed, and referral via the DSL — not through surveillance of families. The DPA 2018 and UK GDPR govern how settings record, share and store personal data; named photographs on a public blog, unlocked safeguarding files and personal phones are common breaches.

Safeguarding legislation and the guidance that operationalises it

  • The Children Act 2004 puts cooperation on a statutory footing so education, health and social care cannot treat a child's safety as someone else's problem. Working Together (2026) is the multi-agency rulebook. It applies to all children, including those in kinship care, looked-after children and unborn children where there are concerns. Family Help (Working Together 2026) combines targeted early help with Children Act 1989 s.17 support so families get a seamless offer, a consistent practitioner relationship and a family help plan led by a multi-disciplinary team, rather than bouncing between 'early help' and 'child in need'. Multi-Agency Safeguarding Arrangements (MASA) are how local partners (local authority, health, police) are held to account. KCSIE (2026, in force 1 September 2026) is the schools and colleges rulebook: all staff must read Part 1 in full; DSL, safer recruitment, allegations, child-on-child abuse, online safety including AI-generated harm, and prejudice-based harm. Early years providers that are not schools follow the EYFS safeguarding and welfare requirements plus Working Together.
  • The Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 underpins DBS and barred-list checks. Children and Families Act 2014 Part 3 rebuilt SEND around a single EHCP. The FGM Act 2003, read with the 2020 multi-agency guidance, requires known under-18 cases to be reported to the police. Supporting pupils at school with medical conditions (2015) requires individual healthcare plans so asthma, diabetes and allergy are planned for, not left to luck.

Equality law and the purpose of the whole framework

  • The Equality Act 2010 makes discrimination, harassment and victimisation unlawful across nine protected characteristics and requires reasonable adjustments for disability. The SEND Code of Practice 0–25 tells early years and schools how to identify need, use a graduated approach and coproduce with families. The UNCRC 1989 is an international treaty, not a UK Act, but it underpins the child's right to protection, education and a voice, and examiners credit it when it is applied rather than name-dropped.
  • Purpose is consistent protection and accountability. Statute without guidance is a duty with no method; guidance without a policy is a document no one can follow on a Tuesday afternoon. Ofsted inspects whether the chain works: Act → statutory guidance → setting policy → staff procedure → what an adult actually does.

Legislation and guidance

  • Health and Safety at Work etc. Act (1974)

    Employers protect employees and the public; employees protect themselves and each other, so far as reasonably practicable.

  • Health and safety: responsibilities and duties for schools (2022)

    Statutory guidance translating HASAWA into school-level duties, risk assessment and first aid.

  • Counter-Terrorism and Security Act (2015)

    Prevent duty on specified authorities, including schools and registered early years providers.

  • Prevent duty guidance for England and Wales (2023)

    How specified authorities implement Prevent, including referral routes.

  • Data Protection Act (2018)

    Fair, lawful, transparent use of personal data; UK GDPR governs recording, sharing and storing.

  • Children Act (2004)

    Multi-agency working and a duty to cooperate to keep children safe.

  • Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act (2006)

    Suitability checks so adults who pose a risk are barred from working with children.

  • Children and Families Act (2014)

    Part 3: SEN system and EHCPs 0–25.

  • Female Genital Mutilation Act (2003)

    Criminalises FGM; mandatory reporting of known cases in under-18s.

  • Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026)

    Statutory multi-agency safeguarding guidance: Family Help, MASA, family group decision-making, anti-discriminatory practice.

  • Keeping Children Safe in Education (2026)

    Statutory safeguarding guidance for schools and colleges; all staff must read Part 1 in full.

  • Equality Act (2010)

    Protected characteristics, reasonable adjustments and the Public Sector Equality Duty.

  • UNCRC (1989)

    Child's rights to protection, education, non-discrimination and a voice.

Exam moves

  • Always pair the Act or guidance with its year and one concrete duty — 'Children Act' or 'safeguarding policy' alone is not AO1.
  • Show the chain: statute → statutory guidance → setting policy → procedure → what you do in the scenario.
  • Match the law to the scenario: wet floor = HASAWA; bruise = Working Together / KCSIE / EYFS; photograph = DPA 2018 / UK GDPR; EHCP = Children and Families Act 2014.
  • Purpose answers need a because: the law exists to protect X, therefore the practitioner must Y.
