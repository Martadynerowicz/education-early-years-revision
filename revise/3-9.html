3.9 Responding to suspected abuse, or a child at risk or in need, using setting procedures

NCFE specification p.51

Core exam · Section B

Core exam

When abuse is suspected, or a child is at risk or in need, the practitioner's job is a sequence, not a hunch. Observe and record as appropriate; follow the setting's child protection policies; follow local safeguarding partnership procedures; use accurate lines of reporting in a timely manner; keep professional boundaries; contact the police if the child is in immediate danger. Do not investigate, do not promise confidentiality, and do not alert the alleged source of harm.

Must know

  • Observe and record as appropriate: date, time, facts, exact words, visible marks described professionally (location, size, colour), who was present. Distinguish observation from opinion. Do not examine the child or photograph on a personal device.
  • Follow organisational child protection policies: named DSL and deputy, cause-for-concern form, what to do if the DSL is absent, whistleblowing if the concern is about a colleague.
  • Follow local safeguarding partnership procedures: thresholds, referral forms, what happens after a referral (Working Together 2026). Education does not run a s.47 enquiry itself.
  • Accurate lines of reporting in a timely manner: the same day, not 'after the weekend' or 'when I have written it up neatly'. Immediate danger = 999.
  • Professional boundaries: no leading questions, no promise of secrecy, no discussion in the staffroom or with other parents, no amateur medical or forensic role.
  • Contact the police if the child is in immediate danger, a crime is in progress, or FGM is known in an under-18 (mandatory reporting under the FGM Act 2003 / Serious Crime Act 2015).
  • If the DSL does not act, the individual still has a duty: follow whistleblowing and, if needed, contact children's social care themselves, then record that they did (KCSIE 2026).

The sequence on the day

  • Listen and stay calm if the child talks. Do not stop them, do not ask 'did X do this?', do not promise that nobody else will be told. Explain that you will need to tell the person whose job is to keep them safe.
  • Record immediately, in the child's words where there is a disclosure. Use a body map only if policy allows and you are describing what you can see without removing clothes. Then tell the DSL or deputy — in person, not only by leaving a note.
  • The DSL uses local partnership thresholds (Working Together 2026; KCSIE 2026; EYFS for non-school providers) to refer to children's social care, and to the police if a crime may have been committed. Information may be shared without consent where necessary to protect a child (DPA 2018 / UK GDPR).

Boundaries, delay and allegations

  • Professional boundaries protect evidence and the child: no home visits to 'find out more', no confronting a parent in the doorway about an injury, no searching a colleague's locker. Allegations about staff go to the head/DSL and LADO.
  • Timely means the same working day. Delay so that a parent is not 'upset at pickup', or because the DSL is on a course, is how harm continues. A deputy DSL and an out-of-hours number exist for this reason.
  • In need and at risk still follow the same first steps (observe, record, tell). The threshold conversation happens with the DSL and social care, not as a reason for a practitioner to wait.

Legislation and guidance

  • Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026)

    Local safeguarding partnership procedures, referral and information sharing.

  • Keeping Children Safe in Education (2026)

    Staff duties, DSL, managing allegations, and referring if the DSL does not act.

  • EYFS statutory framework

    Safeguarding and welfare requirements for registered early years providers, including a child protection policy and a lead practitioner.

  • Data Protection Act (2018)

    Lawful sharing of personal data where necessary to protect a child is not a breach.

  • Female Genital Mutilation Act (2003)

    Mandatory reporting of known FGM in under-18s to the police.

Exam moves

  • Write a sequence: observe/record → setting CP policy → local partnership procedures → timely reporting line → boundaries → 999 if immediate danger.
  • Name the DSL (or deputy) and say 'same day'. Vague 'tell someone' loses marks.
  • State what you will not do: investigate, examine, promise secrecy, alert the alleged abuser.
  • If the scenario is a colleague, switch to allegations / LADO; if it is immediate danger, start with 999.
