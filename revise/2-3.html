2.3 What the EYFS and the national curriculum (KS1 to KS4) cover, and why they exist

NCFE specification p.41

Core exam · Section A (with Element 6)

Core exam

The EYFS covers birth to five (the end of Reception). The National Curriculum then runs through primary key stages 1 and 2 (5–11) and secondary key stages 3 and 4 (11–16). Together they give a common entitlement, continuity at transfer and a basis for assessment. Development Matters is non-statutory guidance that helps practitioners implement the EYFS; it is not the law.

Must know

  • EYFS scope: 0–5 years, from birth to the end of the Reception year, for registered early years providers and Reception classes.
  • EYFS purpose: statutory learning, development and safeguarding/welfare requirements so every young child has a consistent entitlement.
  • Development Matters is non-statutory curriculum guidance that helps practitioners plan from the EYFS educational programmes; it does not replace the statutory framework or the Early Learning Goals.
  • Key stage 1 (Years 1–2, ages 5–7) and key stage 2 (Years 3–6, ages 7–11) make up the primary National Curriculum (5–11).
  • Key stage 3 (Years 7–9, ages 11–14) and key stage 4 (Years 10–11, ages 14–16) make up the secondary National Curriculum (11–16).
  • Purpose of the National Curriculum: a broad, balanced, common entitlement, continuity between schools, and a basis for national assessment (for example phonics screening, KS2 tests, GCSEs).
  • Maintained schools must teach the National Curriculum; academies must teach a broad and balanced curriculum including English, mathematics and science, and most still use National Curriculum programmes of study.
  • Do not apply Early Learning Goals to Year 2, or GCSE specifications to a toddler — match the framework to the child's age.

EYFS 0–5 and Development Matters

  • The EYFS statutory framework sets the educational programmes, the Early Learning Goals used in the EYFS Profile at the end of Reception, the characteristics of effective teaching and learning, and the safeguarding and welfare requirements (including ratios, key person, premises and child protection). It applies to childminders, nurseries, preschools and Reception, so a four-year-old in a PVI nursery and a four-year-old in a school Reception class share the same statutory map.
  • Seven areas of learning comprise three prime areas (communication and language; physical development; personal, social and emotional development) and four specific areas (literacy; mathematics; understanding the world; expressive arts and design). Prime areas are the foundations; specific areas grow from them.
  • Development Matters (currently the 2023 DfE guidance) is non-statutory. It offers checkpoints and examples that help practitioners observe, plan and check typical progress from birth to five. Birth to 5 Matters is similarly guidance from the sector. Exam answers that treat Development Matters as the legal document, or that treat the Early Learning Goals as a curriculum to teach from the baby room, lose marks.

Primary: key stage 1 and key stage 2 (5–11)

  • Once children leave Reception they enter key stage 1. The National Curriculum programmes of study set out what must be taught in maintained schools. Core subjects — English, mathematics and science — run throughout. Foundation subjects include art and design, computing, design and technology, geography, history, music and PE, with a modern foreign language from key stage 2. RE and relationships education sit alongside via separate statute.
  • Purpose at primary is mastery of the basics, a broad experience of the disciplines, and comparable assessment: the Year 1 phonics screening check, the Year 4 multiplication tables check and key stage 2 national tests and teacher assessment in Year 6. These outcomes inform the Year 7 teacher and national accountability; they are not the whole curriculum.
  • A Year 2 teacher planning reading therefore uses the National Curriculum English programme of study and the school's phonics programme, not the EYFS educational programmes. Children who were 'emerging' against ELGs still need a KS1 curriculum that builds from that starting point, with SEND support where needed.

Secondary: key stage 3 and key stage 4 (11–16)

  • Key stage 3 (11–14) continues a broad National Curriculum, adding citizenship and a compulsory modern foreign language alongside the cores and foundations. Its purpose is to deepen subject knowledge before pupils specialise. Key stage 4 (14–16) retains English, mathematics, science, computing, PE and citizenship, with religious education and RSHE, while options — often including EBacc subjects such as history, geography and languages — lead to GCSEs and equivalent qualifications regulated by Ofqual.
  • Scope stops at 16 for this specification point. Post-16 study programmes (A levels, T Levels, apprenticeships) are not a continuation of the National Curriculum. A 15-year-old in Year 10 is KS4; a 12-year-old in Year 7 is KS3; neither is in the EYFS.
  • Purpose across KS1–KS4 is entitlement and coherence: a child who moves from a community primary to an academy secondary should not lose science or PE because of postcode. Inspection then judges how well that entitlement is implemented for all pupils, including those with SEND and those who speak English as an additional language.

Legislation and guidance

  • Education Reform Act (1988)

    Introduced the first National Curriculum and national assessment in England.

  • Education Act (2002)

    Requires maintained schools to deliver the National Curriculum and a broad, balanced curriculum.

  • Childcare Act (2006)

    Underpins the requirement for registered early years providers to deliver the EYFS.

  • EYFS statutory framework (2024)

    Sets the statutory learning, development and welfare requirements for 0–5.

Exam moves

  • Name the three prime and four specific areas in full if asked — initials alone rarely score.
  • State that Development Matters is non-statutory whenever a question contrasts guidance with the framework.
  • Match the age in the scenario: Reception = EYFS; Year 4 = KS2; Year 8 = KS3; Year 11 = KS4.
  • For purpose questions write entitlement, consistency/continuity and assessment — not a history essay unless asked.
