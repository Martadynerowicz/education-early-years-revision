1.2 General, specialist and support roles in early years and education, and what each person does

NCFE specification p.40

Primarily the ESP — may also appear in the core exam

ESP

The sector is organised around general classroom and room-based roles, specialist posts that carry a whole-setting duty, and supporting professionals who are usually employed outside the setting. Exam answers must match the adult to a unique responsibility: an early years educator is not a teacher, a DSL is not a SENDCo, and the LADO is not the child's social worker. Apply the role to the setting in the scenario — nursery language for 0–5, teacher and TA language for school.

Must know

  • General roles: early years practitioner, early years educator, room leader, teaching assistant, teacher, headteacher.
  • An early years practitioner (typically level 2) supports care, play and routines under supervision and does not fill the level 3 staff:child ratio on their own.
  • An early years educator (level 3, full and relevant) can count in EYFS ratios, plans and observes, and often acts as key person.
  • Room leaders coordinate a room or age band; teaching assistants support teaching, interventions and inclusion under the teacher; teachers are accountable for curriculum, assessment and behaviour; the headteacher holds overall leadership of the setting.
  • Specialist roles: SENDCo (SEND Code of Practice 2015); DSL (Keeping Children Safe in Education); PANCo (physical activity and nutrition in early years); mental health first aider (MHFA).
  • Supporting roles: speech and language therapist; health visitor; social worker; GP; LADO; educational psychologist.
  • The LADO (Local Authority Designated Officer) advises on allegations about adults who work with children; they are not the practitioner who delivers the child's daily care.
  • Supporting professionals usually visit or advise; the setting still holds day-to-day safeguarding and learning duties and must work in partnership with them.

General roles in early years and schools

  • An early years practitioner works under direction: care routines, play, observations and support for the key person. An early years educator holds a full and relevant level 3 qualification, counts in ratios, plans sequences of play and learning against the EYFS, completes assessments and is often the named key person for a group of children. A room leader (or room supervisor) sets the daily rhythm of a baby, toddler or preschool room, deploys staff, models practice and escalates safeguarding or SEND concerns to the right lead.
  • In schools, a teaching assistant scaffolds learning, runs interventions (for example phonics or speech), supports pupils with SEND and must not be used as a substitute teacher. The class teacher holds qualified teacher status in maintained schools and is accountable for progress, adaptive teaching, assessment and behaviour in that class. The headteacher is the setting's lead professional: vision, staffing, budget, standards and overall accountability, including that a DSL is in post and that safeguarding policy is followed.
  • In the exam, match the job title to a duty the others do not hold. A teaching assistant notices and reports; a teacher plans the sequence of lessons; a headteacher is accountable for the whole school. Do not write that every adult in a nursery is a teacher.

Specialist roles: SENDCo, DSL, PANCo and MHFA

  • The SENDCo (or SENCO) coordinates the graduated approach (assess, plan, do, review), the SEND register and Education, Health and Care Plan processes, and advises colleagues on adaptations. In maintained mainstream schools the role has specified training expectations (the NPQ for SENCOs, replacing NASENCO). The designated safeguarding lead is the named person for child protection concerns, referrals to children's social care, confidential records and staff training under Keeping Children Safe in Education. Every adult still has a duty to notice and report; only the DSL coordinates the setting's response.
  • The PANCo (Physical Activity and Nutrition Coordinator) is an early years specialist role. They champion healthy eating, physical activity, related policies and staff knowledge so that food, movement and outdoor play are consistent across rooms. They are not a dietitian and they do not replace the DSL or SENDCo.
  • A mental health first aider (MHFA) is trained to spot signs of mental ill-health, listen non-judgementally, give initial support and signpost to the senior mental health lead, GP or specialist services. They are not a counsellor or therapist and they must not keep a disclosure of harm confidential from the DSL. In a scenario, if a Year 9 pupil talks about feeling unable to cope, the MHFA listens and passes on; they do not start a treatment plan.

Supporting roles beyond the setting

  • Speech and language therapists assess and treat speech, language and communication needs and give the setting strategies (for example visual supports or targeted vocabulary). Health visitors lead the Healthy Child Programme for 0–5, including developmental reviews, and are often the first professional to flag a delay to a nursery. GPs assess health, make referrals and share relevant medical information with consent or under safeguarding procedures.
  • Social workers act under the Children Act 1989 and Children Act 2004 for children in need and children who may be at risk of significant harm. They lead statutory assessment and child protection processes; the setting contributes information and follows the child protection plan. Educational psychologists advise on cognition, learning, development and the child's educational needs, often contributing psychological advice to an EHCP. They do not replace the teacher's daily assessment.
  • The LADO sits in the local authority and manages allegations against people who work with children. If a parent alleges that a member of staff has behaved in a way that may have harmed a child, the DSL or headteacher contacts the LADO for advice; staff do not investigate on their own or alert the accused in a way that could compromise the process. Keep exam answers procedural: name the LADO, the DSL and the duty to report, without graphic detail.

Legislation and guidance

  • EYFS statutory framework (2024)

    Sets which qualifications are full and relevant for counting in early years staff:child ratios and requires providers to have regard to safeguarding and welfare.

  • SEND Code of Practice (2015)

    Requires an identified SENDCo and sets out the graduated approach and partnership with specialists.

  • Keeping Children Safe in Education (2026)

    Requires a designated safeguarding lead and sets out staff duties to report concerns.

  • Children Act (1989)

    Underpins social work duties for children in need (s.17) and child protection (s.47), and the wider safeguarding partnership including the LADO process.

  • Children and Families Act (2014)

    Introduced EHCPs and strengthened multi-agency duties, including input from educational psychologists and health professionals.

Exam moves

  • Match the role to a unique duty: DSL for referrals, SENDCo for the graduated approach, PANCo for nutrition and activity, LADO for allegations about adults, MHFA for initial mental-health support and signposting.
  • Do not treat an early years educator as a qualified teacher, or EYTS as QTS, unless the question is about teacher status — this point is roles and responsibilities, not career routes.
  • Apply the setting: nursery answers should use EYE, room leader, PANCo and health visitor; Year 5 answers should use TA, teacher, SENDCo and educational psychologist.
  • For supporting roles, say what they contribute and that the setting still holds day-to-day duty — listing job titles with no function scores little.
