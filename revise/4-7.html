4.7 How disability and cognitive impairment affect language, communication, function and emotions

NCFE specification p.54

Core exam · Section C

Core exam

Disabilities and cognitive impairments can change how a child understands and uses language, how they function day to day, and how they feel. The specification influences are: comprehension of reading, writing and maths; vocabulary and communication; attention span; coordination; logical reasoning; memory and building on prior knowledge; perception; motor skills and mobility. Each academic or functional hit can then feed anxiety, frustration or withdrawal. Influence is not destiny: AAC, scaffolding and environmental change reduce the impact.

Must know

  • Comprehension of reading, writing and maths: decoding, comprehension, recording and number sense all load language, memory, visual processing and reasoning. Impairment in any of those skills changes access to the curriculum.
  • Vocabulary and communication: a smaller or differently organised vocabulary, delayed expressive language, or unclear speech affects learning and friendships. Communication includes non-verbal and AAC, not only talk.
  • Attention span: shorter or more fluctuating attention reduces how much teaching sticks and how long the child can stay in play or a task.
  • Coordination: planning and executing movement (dyspraxia / DCD and other profiles) affects writing, dressing, PE and using classroom tools.
  • Logical reasoning: difficulty with sequences, inference and 'if… then…' hits problem-solving, behaviour choices and maths.
  • Memory and building on prior knowledge: if yesterday's learning cannot be retrieved, every lesson starts at zero. That is exhausting and emotionally flattening.
  • Perception: mis-seeing or mis-hearing the room, the print or the social cue (a facial expression) changes function and relationships.
  • Motor skills and mobility: getting to the lesson, sitting, writing, accessing the toilet and joining play. Function is independence in the real building, not only test scores.

Language, communication and the curriculum

  • Reading, writing and maths are language-heavy. A child with DLD, a hearing impairment, or a learning disability may have the idea and not the sentence, or the calculation and not the word problem. Assessment must separate 'does not know' from 'cannot show it in this format'.
  • Vocabulary and communication difficulties isolate. Peers move fast; adults over-question; the child stops offering ideas. AAC, extra time, and talking partners are functional supports, not extras.
  • Attention, memory and reasoning sit underneath comprehension. A child who cannot hold a sentence in working memory will look as if they cannot read for meaning. Teach the cognitive skill and adapt the task.

Function, mobility and emotional development

  • Coordination, motor skills and mobility decide whether the child can keep up with the hidden curriculum: coat, bag, lining up, getting to the next room, sitting on the carpet, using the toilets that happen to be upstairs. Fatigue from this work leaves less for learning.
  • Perception and mobility also affect safety and confidence outdoors. A child who cannot judge depth or who uses a walker needs the environment changed (inclusion), not a constant 'be careful' that shrinks their world.
  • Emotional development: repeated public struggle, not being understood, and being left out produce frustration, anxiety and sometimes 'behaviour'. Name the influence, adapt, and keep expectation. The Equality Act 2010 requires that these influences are not allowed to become substantial disadvantage.

Legislation and guidance

  • Equality Act (2010)

    Reasonable adjustments so impairments do not substantially disadvantage curriculum access, communication or mobility.

  • SEND Code of Practice 0–25

    Cognition and learning, communication and interaction, sensory and/or physical, SEMH — needs overlap; provision should too.

  • Children and Families Act (2014)

    EHCP provision can include therapies and equipment that support function and communication.

Exam moves

  • Pick three specification influences and apply them to language/communication, function (daily skills) and emotional development — the title has all three.
  • Do not write a medical essay. Stay with educational impact and what the practitioner does.
  • Show a chain: memory difficulty → cannot build on prior knowledge → falls behind → anxiety.
  • Mention AAC or environmental change so the answer is not deficit-only.
