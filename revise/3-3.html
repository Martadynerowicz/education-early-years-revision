3.3 Managing and reporting risk to keep children safe and well

NCFE specification p.48

Core exam · Section B

Core exam

Risk management is a cycle, not a one-off form. The five steps are: identify hazards; determine who is at risk; evaluate the risks and select mitigations; record the findings and implement them; monitor and review. In education and early years this covers premises, activities, outings, individual children (allergy, SEND, medical needs) and safeguarding risks, and findings must be reported through the setting's named lines.

Must know

  • Step 1 — Identify hazards: anything with the potential to cause harm (wet floor, unsecured gate, unlabelled cleaning fluid, uncollected child, unsupervised one-to-one, online contact, a known medical need without a plan).
  • Step 2 — Determine who is at risk: children (including babies, children with SEND, visitors, pregnant staff, contractors). Risk is not the same for every person.
  • Step 3 — Evaluate risks and select mitigations: likelihood × severity; then control measures (remove, reduce, isolate, train, PPE, supervision, ratios, locked stores, visual signs).
  • Step 4 — Record findings and implement: written risk assessment, individual healthcare plan, outing form, or cause-for-concern form — then actually do the controls, not file them.
  • Step 5 — Monitor and review: after an incident, when a child or activity changes, at the date set on the form, and when guidance changes. A risk assessment from 2019 is not current.
  • Reporting risk is part of the duty: tell the named person (manager, site lead, DSL, first aider) in a timely way. Students and TAs do not 'leave it' because they are not in charge.
  • HASAWA 1974, the 2022 schools duties guidance, EYFS welfare requirements and Working Together / KCSIE all require this cycle. Dynamic (on-the-spot) risk assessment sits alongside written assessments — for example stopping an activity when a storm starts.

The five steps in an early years or school context

  • Identify hazards by walking the environment and by knowing the children: a trailing cable, a broken fence, a child who bolts, a visitor without a DBS-supervised status, a social-media request from a parent. Hazards include safeguarding and wellbeing, not only slips and trips.
  • Who is at risk changes the control. A four-year-old near a pond, a child with a severe allergy at snack, a non-mobile baby on a play mat, a pregnant colleague lifting chairs — the same room holds different risks for different people.
  • Evaluate and mitigate using the hierarchy of control. Removing the hazard (not having a lit candle on a birthday cake in a toddler room) is stronger than a sign. Supervision and ratios are mitigations, not a substitute for a safe environment. Record the residual risk honestly.

Record, implement, monitor — and report

  • A risk assessment that is not implemented is theatre. The locked COSHH cupboard, the two-person outing, the individual healthcare plan for the child with asthma, and the visitor badge system are the assessment in action.
  • Monitor after near-misses as well as accidents. If three children slip in the same cloakroom, the control has failed. Review dates, new children, new equipment and new statutory guidance (a KCSIE update, a revised EYFS requirement) all trigger a fresh look.
  • Reporting: premises hazards go to the site/manager; child-level medical risk to the first aider and key person; safeguarding risk to the DSL the same day; immediate danger to the police on 999. Lines of reporting must be in the procedure so a student knows who to tell if the manager is off site.

Legislation and guidance

  • Health and Safety at Work etc. Act (1974)

    Requires risk to be assessed and controlled so far as reasonably practicable.

  • Health and safety: responsibilities and duties for schools (2022)

    Expects written risk assessments, implementation and review.

  • EYFS statutory framework

    Providers must take all necessary steps to keep children safe and well, including premises, outings and risk assessment.

  • Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026)

    Safeguarding risk is reported through local procedures, not handled as a private worry.

Exam moves

  • Write the five steps in order. Examiners penalise 'do a risk assessment' with no steps.
  • Apply each step to the scenario's hazard (pond, outing, allergy, unlocked gate) rather than reciting a generic list.
  • Finish with who you would report to and when — manage and report are both in the heading.
  • Distinguish hazard (the thing) from risk (the chance and severity of harm to a named group).
