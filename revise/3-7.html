3.7 Grooming and exploitation: signs and possible outcomes

NCFE specification p.50

Core exam · Section B

Core exam

Grooming is the process of building a relationship, trust and an emotional connection so a child can be manipulated, exploited and abused. Exploitation is the outcome: the child is used. Specification outcomes include taking advantage of the child, unauthorised access to private or sensitive information, manipulation, using trusted status to bully, shame or undermine, and threatening punishment for non-compliance. Signs include a change from typical behaviour, withdrawal or isolation, inability to concentrate, and sexualised behaviour, language or understanding that is not age-appropriate.

Must know

  • Grooming is a process (target, build trust, isolate, secrets, test boundaries, exploit), not a single event. It can be sexual, criminal or ideological, online or in person, by adults or by other children.
  • Outcomes — taking advantage: using the child for sexual, criminal, domestic or online purposes, or for the adult's emotional needs, because the child cannot freely refuse.
  • Outcomes — unauthorised access to private or sensitive information: passwords, images, family details, safeguarding or medical information, which can then be used to coerce ('I will share that picture').
  • Outcomes — manipulation, and using trusted status to bully, shame or undermine: the same power that should protect is used to shame the child into compliance.
  • Outcomes — threatening punishment for non-compliance: sanctions, exposure, harm to family, or withdrawal of the 'special' relationship if the child does not do as they are told.
  • Signs: changes in typical behaviour; withdrawn or isolated; inability to concentrate; sexualised behaviour, language or understanding that is not age-appropriate. Also (applied): unexplained gifts, a new phone, missing episodes, defending an older 'friend'.
  • Children cannot consent to their own exploitation. 'They wanted to go' or 'they sent the picture' is the grooming talking, not a professional conclusion. Response: DSL the same day; do not confront the suspected groomer; record; follow KCSIE / local exploitation pathways.

Outcomes: what grooming is for

  • Taking advantage and manipulation are the core: the relationship was never equal. Gifts, attention and secrets are the bait; the child is then used.
  • Unauthorised access to private or sensitive information is both a method and an outcome. Once a groomer has images, messages or family facts, shame and threats of exposure keep the child compliant. Settings that are sloppy with passwords, displays of named photographs or unlocked files make that easier.
  • Using a position of trust to bully, humiliate or undermine, and threatening punishment for non-compliance, show that exploitation is not only 'stranger danger'. A coach, tutor, older pupil or family friend can harm through the role itself. Victim-blaming repeats the groomer's logic.

Signs and the professional response

  • Change from that child's baseline is the most useful sign: a sociable child becomes withdrawn and isolated; concentration collapses; language or behaviour becomes sexualised in a way that does not match their stage. Clusters matter more than one off-day.
  • Online and extra-familial harm (criminal or sexual exploitation) may add unexplained money, phones, missing from education, or a new controlling contact. KCSIE (2026) requires schools to treat this as abuse.
  • Do not confront the suspected person, do not confiscate devices carelessly (evidence), do not promise secrecy, and do not delay because the child says they are 'fine'. Tell the DSL. Sexual Offences Act 2003 s.15 covers meeting a child following sexual grooming; the professional duty starts at the first indicator.

Legislation and guidance

  • Sexual Offences Act (2003)

    Includes meeting a child following sexual grooming (s.15) and position of trust offences.

  • Keeping Children Safe in Education (2026)

    Child sexual and criminal exploitation, online safety and extra-familial harm.

  • Working Together to Safeguard Children (2026)

    Multi-agency response to exploitation and extra-familial harm.

Exam moves

  • Define grooming as a process, then name two specification outcomes and two specification signs — do not only write 'being nice then abusing'.
  • Reject victim-blaming; name power and the child's lack of true consent.
  • Apply online and in-person; finish with DSL, no confrontation, record.
  • Keep sexualised-behaviour points professional and non-graphic.
