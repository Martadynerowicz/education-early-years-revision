Scenarios

Write as you would on assignment 3 or in a professional discussion. Reveal the model after you have attempted it. Early Years Educator placements need at least 750 hours.

  • stretchButtercup Room · 2–3s · 24 children, 4 staff

    A disclosure at the dough table

    You are the student on a Thursday morning. Maisie, 2 years 10 months, is at the dough table. While you are sitting at her level she says, quietly, that Daddy hurts Mummy when it is dark and that she has to stay in her room. She shows you a faded bruise on her upper arm and says 'don't tell, it's a secret'. Your mentor is changing a nappy. The room is busy. Maisie's father is due to collect at 1pm. You have been taught that you are not a DSL and that you must not promise secrecy.

  • secureOak Class · Reception · 30 children

    Noah and the block throw

    Noah, 4 years 7 months, has an identified speech delay and recently started living with his grandmother. During construction play he screams and throws a wooden block that misses another child by centimetres. Two children freeze. Your mentor is with a parent at the door. You are the nearest adult. Noah's face is red; he is breathing fast and does not respond to his name. There is a positive handling plan on file that lists 'loud carpets and having models knocked' as triggers and asks adults to reduce language, offer space, and use a calm script: 'Noah, blocks stay down. I am here.' Physical intervention is not part of his plan except to move others if they are in the path.

  • secureWillow Nursery · preschool · 16 children

    Amira and the birthday party

    Amira, 3 years 9 months, is autistic. She wears ear defenders, uses a now/next board and has a one-page profile that says she loves water play and finds singing on the carpet overwhelming. Staff have planned a 'surprise' birthday circle with a loud speaker and hats for another child. Amira's mother has asked that Amira never be taken out of the room 'so she learns to cope'. A visiting student suggests putting Amira in the office with a tablet 'so the others can enjoy it'. You are helping to set up.

  • foundationReception, Greenfield Primary

    Yusuf's first weeks

    Yusuf, 5, arrived from Sudan six weeks ago. He is in the silent (pre-production) period: he watches, complies with routines, and has started to smile at a peer who also speaks Arabic. A TA says 'he needs to speak English all day or he will never learn' and has been removing his home-language picture book. Yusuf's mother has little English and works nights. You notice Yusuf knows the visual timetable and can fetch his coat when shown the photo, but he has not yet spoken in the classroom.

  • secureSunflower Room · 3–4s

    The parent who never comes in

    Leah's dad, Tom, works early shifts in a warehouse and has his own unhappy memories of school. He drops Leah at 7.45 at the gate and does not come in. Staff have labelled him 'not interested'. Leah's learning journal is on an app he has not logged into. You overhear a colleague say they will 'deal with it at parents' evening — if he bothers'. Leah is making strong progress in the garden and you have photos of her building a water channel. Tom's English is fluent; he just never crosses the threshold.

  • foundationAcorn Room · 2–3s

    The transporting bags

    For two weeks, Imani has filled every available bag, basket and bucket and carried it to the home corner, emptied it, and started again. A colleague keeps putting the bags away 'because she is making a mess'. You have been asked to complete a written observation for your placement file.

  • foundationGarden, Little Oaks · mixed 2–4s

    Split lip on the climbing frame

    After lunch, Kajal, 3, jumps from the second rung of the climbing frame, catches her foot and splits her lip. There is a lot of blood, she is crying, and two children are watching. You are first aid trained at paediatric level. The accident book is in the office. Kajal's mother listed no allergies; tetanus status is unknown. The climbing frame was checked at opening; the ground is bark that has thinned at the jump-off point.

  • stretchYear 1, community primary

    A drawing that worries you

    During drawing, Callum, 6, produces a picture of 'the baddies my uncle showed me on his phone' with flags you do not recognise and the words 'they will get us'. He is cheerful and wants to tell the class at show-and-tell. The school's Prevent duty is displayed in the staffroom. You have been told students must not investigate families. Callum's uncle collects him on Fridays.

  • secureDaisy Room · 3–4s

    Starting again, again

    Tyler, 3 years 5 months, is looked after and has moved to a new foster household 40 minutes away. He starts with you on Monday after two previous settings this year. He carries a torn photograph of his previous key person and becomes distressed when it is time to go outside without it. His social worker wants 'a full baseline by Friday'. His foster carer looks exhausted at the door.

  • stretchAfter-school club, 5–8s

    The volunteer who always has sweets

    A new volunteer, 'Uncle Ray', is a grandparent of a child in the club. He brings sweets for 'his favourites', asks to walk children to the toilet alone, and has added three families on Facebook. Two children compete for his lap. A colleague laughs that he is 'just a big kid'. You have read the setting's safer-recruitment and social-media policy. You are a student, not the manager.

  • foundationSnack time, 3–4s room

    Five cups, four children

    You are preparing snack. There are five cups and four children at the table; one child is washing hands. Your mentor wants you to 'get maths into the routine' without turning snack into a test. Two children have EAL; one avoids counting aloud.

  • securePre-school, inner-city PVI

    December in a diverse room

    The room team has always done a nativity and a visit from 'Santa'. This year the cohort includes families who are Muslim, Hindu, Jehovah's Witness, and two families with no religion. A staff member says 'it's British culture, they chose to come here'. A parent has emailed to ask that their child not take part in worship. You are planning a December display.
